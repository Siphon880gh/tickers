Before you trade: a simple research checklist
  1. Know what you own. A stock is a business. A stock ETF holds a basket of businesses. Bullion trusts and futures funds have different structures and risks.
  2. Check the business or fund. For a company, read revenue, profit, debt, cash flow, and management guidance. For a fund, check its issuer, holdings, fees, index, concentration, and prospectus.
  3. Look for the next catalyst. Earnings, inflation reports, central-bank decisions, inventory reports, and supply changes can matter. Separate an announced event from speculation.
  4. Check the market price. Valuation, trading volume, the bid–ask spread, and past price swings help you understand what a trade may cost. A good story can already be priced in.
  5. Cross-check the conversation. Social sentiment reflects the people posting, not every investor. Compare claims with filings and primary sources.
  6. Define your downside. Decide how much loss you can tolerate before choosing a position size. Understand your order type and any tax consequences.

ETF = exchange-traded fund. A ticker is the short symbol used to identify a security on an exchange. The examples here are research starting points, not buy or sell recommendations.
